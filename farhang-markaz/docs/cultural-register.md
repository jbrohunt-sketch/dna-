# Farhang Markaz — Cultural Claims Register (v3)

Maintained by the Central Asia Cultural Auditor. Every claim that changes the design is recorded
with **confidence** (High / Med / Low), **exclusivity** (EXCL = excludes alternatives · STRONG =
strongly associated · SHARED = common to several traditions), the **strongest source** with its
quality, and **conflicting evidence**. The skill file states only the design consequence and
cites these IDs. Goal: specificity, not brittle certainty.

Source quality: primary · academic · museum · secondary · tourism · commercial.

## Rishtan plate structure (for abstraction)

- **Documented** ([Q], [R]): separate **centre** and **rim** zones outlined in dark, near-black
  lines; centres = concentric floral rosettes, solar motifs, cinquefoil medallion, or an
  oftoba ewer; middle field = **radiating almond-shaped leaves (bodom), each in its own
  compartment**, or islimi scrolls; rims = symmetrical palmettes, a **checked band**, or
  concentric geometric/foliate bands.
- **Colour order:** milky-white ground → dark brown/manganese outline → cobalt → translucent
  turquoise/green ishkor.
- **Four-lobed centre:** four-lobed centres are documented on a 19th-c. "probably Kokand" dish
  (V&A O225424). Deriving our centre from the doppi's four bushes is a **design rhyme, not a
  cultural link**.
- **Segment count: no canonical number** (4- and 5-fold documented). Our 12 compartments
  (= skirt panels = warp columns, for shared-count morphing) is a **design choice**.
- **Mostly floral**; geometric ornament historically minor ([Y], tourism, Low-Med).
- **Avoid** (reads as Iznik or religious): raised tomato red, tulip/carnation/saz leaf,
  central star, calligraphy band, mehrob (niche) motif.
- **Modern conventions** (Low): all-cobalt "Rishtan blue" plates with pomegranate centres.

## Chamanda gul (headpiece) — evidence is thin

- **Documented** [A]: square women's cap, iroki embroidery, "four identical motifs of chamanda
  gul (flowers on a small grassy clearing)": green bushes, bright pink flowers, black outline;
  Margilan, 15.5 × 15.5 cm, cotton + velvet.
- **Inferred (Low-Med):** one motif per face of the square crown, rotated 90°. Ground colour undocumented.
- **Not documented:** flowers per bush.
- **Abstraction used:** rounded square with a band border; four identical bushes at the
  edge midpoints, stems toward the centre; 3 pink flowers + ishkor (standing in for green)
  leaves per bush; **milk ground** (stylisation — the dark ground failed the 0.1× thumbnail
  test and read as the black men's cap [R1]). Flower count and ground colour are stylisation.

## Design decisions recorded at styleframe approval (rev 7)

- **Nimcha omitted** (stylisation). R6 remains the reference if a garment layer returns.
- **Ikat label:** "abrbandi (khan-atlas-derived)" — the drawn motif is a symmetric stepped
  flame on threads; plain "khan-atlas" slightly overclaims [R5].
- **Braids:** one swept ribbon of 6 braids reads as long hair at thumbnail; claim qirq kokil
  only where separate strands are visible [R3].
- **Skirt bundle count = 5** (odd): avoids a 4-fold rotational, swastika-adjacent read.
- **Identity:** solid disc + doppi tile; describe the cap as one documented women's cap type,
  not *the* Fergana symbol [R2].

## Design decisions recorded after the Phase 2 gate

- Morph 3 (stepped ikat flame → smooth bodom leaf) is a **formal rhyme** between shared
  pointed-oval shapes, not a claim of lineage. If *bodom* is confirmed as a named khan-atlas /
  abrbandi motif, it becomes a documented bridge (open item 4).
- Doppi ground = milk (stylisation), chosen because the dark ground failed the 0.1× thumbnail
  test and read as the black men's cap [R1].

## Open verification items

- Whether *bodom* is a named khan-atlas or abrbandi motif (would make Morph 3 a documented bridge).
- A Rishtan-attributed museum lagan (e.g. Warsaw Museum of Asia and the Pacific album; State
  Museum of Applied Arts, Tashkent) to confirm plate composition.
- A photo of the Náprstek chamanda gul cap: motif placement and ground colour.

- Photos of Mukarram Turgunbayeva / Bahor ensemble Fergana dances (e.g. *Andijon polkasi*):
  which skullcap type do female dancers wear on stage? (affects R1 confidence)
- Braid counts in stage costume (R3).
